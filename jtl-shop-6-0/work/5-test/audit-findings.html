S1

Dashboard

The merchant's home screen: a five-task go-live checklist in the right rail, multi-shop management, team & 2FA, and global payment settings. Ten criteria fail here, and the two heaviest are about what the checklist leaves out.

Source · Shop Setup Platform-10.08.2026.html · rendered 1512×950

S1-01W3.1 · W3.2 Needs fixing 1 pinned
“Can a merchant reach ‘live’ with any legally required element still unset?”

The checklist is Create your shop · Customize your theme · Set up payments · Add a shipping zone · Connect a custom domain. Consent management, legal notice, withdrawal instructions, tax configuration and price-display mode appear nowhere in it — and the specification makes all of them mandatory for DE/EU operation. A merchant can reach 5 of 5, watch the counter complete, and be non-compliant on day one.

All five tasks also carry identical styling and identical Set up affordances, so nothing separates “you legally must” from “you probably want to”. Worse, the panel's own copy reads “Toggle a task to mark it complete” — progress reflects what the merchant ticked, not what is actually configured.

S1-05H9.5 · H5.3 Needs fixing 2 pinned
“Do warnings distinguish ‘this will fail’ from ‘this is unusual but allowed’?”

The toggle reads Require 2FA for all admins and is switched on. Two rows below it — Leo Pham (Staff) and Studio Bright (External) — show 2FA Off. Either the rule is not enforced, or “admins” silently excludes those roles. The screen defines neither and raises no warning about the apparent contradiction. For a security control, a state the user cannot resolve is worse than no control at all.

S1-03H3.5 · H5.5 · H3.2 Needs fixing 3 pinned
“Are irreversible actions separated from routine ones so they can't be hit by accident?”

Each member's role is an inline Owner | Staff | External segmented control and each 2FA state is a clickable pill. Both are wired to instant-apply handlers in the markup, with no confirmation step — and there is no Undo control anywhere in the file. Switching off a colleague's 2FA, or demoting the only Owner, is one stray click in a table that otherwise reads as static content.

The same segmented control is a passive selector everywhere else in the product. Same component, entirely different consequence, no visual distinction.

S1-04W1.1 · W1.3 · H1.5 Needs fixing 4 pinned
“When a value is a synced mirror, is the last sync time or freshness shown?”

The table shows status, URL and team size per storefront. Nothing on the screen mentions the WaWi connection: no last-sync time, no sync health, no marker distinguishing ERP-derived values from shop-owned ones. On a platform whose central premise is ERP-led data, the merchant's home screen cannot answer “is my data current?”

Copy nit in the same card: “Manage every frontend, its configuration and assigned team” — developer vocabulary aimed at an explicitly non-technical audience, when storefront is already the word used elsewhere in the product.

S1-10H2.5 · H4.1 Needs improvement 5 pinned
“Are numbers, dates, currencies and units in the format the user's market expects?”

The global search placeholder is German inside an interface that is otherwise entirely English. Since shop admin is specified to support DE and EN, the question this raises is which language the admin is actually in — and whether the answer follows the operator or the storefront. Elsewhere on the same screen, “1 members” is unpluralised.

S2

Add shop

Creating a storefront takes three fields. The brevity is the design's best idea and its biggest gap: what is missing from the modal decides the new shop's legal and tax posture without the merchant ever seeing it.

S2-01W3.4 · W3.3 Needs fixing 8 pinned
“Do market-specific requirements follow the shop's country, not the admin's UI language?”

The modal collects a name, a category and an optional domain. There is no country, market, currency or language field — yet the prototype's own state holds language: “German”, taxIncluded: true as defaults. Every new storefront silently inherits a German, tax-inclusive configuration its owner was never shown and never chose.

For a product that names European expansion as a core requirement, market belongs at creation time. It determines price display, tax rules and which legal texts are mandatory — three things that are expensive to discover after launch.

S2-02H2.1 · W1.1 Needs fixing 6 pinned
“Is it visible, per field, whether this value comes from WaWi or lives in the shop?”

The middle field reads only Select category. That these are WaWi categories, and that this one selection determines which products the storefront carries, sits behind a tooltip. It is the most consequential decision in the modal and the least explained — the specification's own phrasing, “fetch products from ERP by categories”, is clearer than the interface's.

The plural in that phrase matters too: the control is a single select, so a storefront spanning several WaWi categories has no path through this modal.

S2-05H5.4 · H3.1 · H1.4 Good, with one gap 7 pinned
“Do defaults lead to a safe, sensible outcome for someone who changes nothing?”

Deferring the domain is the strongest decision in the flow. Pointing an A record is the step most likely to need outside help, and blocking on it would stall everything behind it. The microcopy says so plainly, in the place the question arises.

The gap is what happens next: nothing states what Create shop produces. A draft? A live storefront? A background product sync of unknown duration? The modal presents itself as one self-contained action when it is the first step of a longer sequence.

S3

Theme setup

The three-panel editor: AI prompt and layer tree on the left, storefront preview centre, element inspector right. Twelve criteria fail here — concentrated on scope, reversibility, and an AI panel that cannot be started.

S3-01W2.3 · W2.5 Needs fixing 10 pinned
“Is inherited-versus-overridden distinguishable at a glance, per property?”

The inspector for Announcement bar shows Font Subheading, Weight Default, Letter spacing Normal, Case Default. Nothing marks whether a value is inherited from the global theme or overridden here. The prototype does contain an Inherits global state and a Customized badge with a reset control — but that reset is a 24 px icon button whose only label is a title tooltip, and none of it appears in the panel as shown.

This is the criterion the whole cascade rests on. A merchant who cannot see what is inherited cannot predict what a global change will do, and the inheritance model stops being a feature.

S3-02W2.4 · W2.2 · H4.1 Needs fixing 11 pinned
“Before a global change lands, is the blast radius named (‘affects 4 storefronts’)?”

In a product where theme values cascade across storefronts, Publish is unqualified. The merchant cannot tell whether they are changing this shop or every shop that inherits the value they just edited — and there is no confirmation step to ask.

The vocabulary drifts too: this build says Save and Publish, the header says Last publish, the status chip says Draft, and the build captured on the board labels the same action Apply to storefront. Pick apply/revert or publish/draft and use one of them.

S3-04H1.2 · H1.4 · H3.2 · H10.1 Needs fixing 9 pinned
“Does every action that takes more than a moment show that it's working?”

The AI theme setup panel offers a textarea — “Describe your brand or paste a reference link…” — and an Import image button. There is no submit control, no processing state, and no summary of what changed once it has run. The most novel interaction on the screen has no visible way to start it and no way to see what it did.

Reversibility compounds it. A prompt can restyle many properties at once, and the file contains no Undo. The version dropdown implies recoverable history, which is the right instinct — but picking a past version is not the same as undoing the change you just watched happen.

S3-06W3.1 · W3.5 Needs fixing
“Can a merchant reach ‘live’ with any legally required element still unset?”

The rendered footer contains one line: © 2026 L'alouette · All rights reserved. No Impressum, no privacy link, no withdrawal policy, and no consent banner anywhere in the preview. Nothing in the publish path checks for the mandatory DACH/EU elements before the storefront goes out.

This is the same failure as S1-01 seen from the other end: the checklist never asks for these, and the publish step never blocks on them.

S3-07H2.5 · H4.1 Needs fixing 12 pinned
“Are numbers, dates, currencies and units in the format the user's market expects?”

The header reads Last publish 11/12/2026 — ambiguous between 11 December and 12 November, in a DACH-first product where the two conventions collide daily. The toolbar directly below uses a different format entirely (Aug 06, 2026, 04:42 PM), and shows an earlier date than the “last publish” of a shop still marked Draft.

S3-10H7.2 · H4.4 · W1.1 Needs improvement 13 pinned
“Does the screen follow the platform conventions users bring from other tools?”

The preview offers two device widths, Desktop and Mobile. The project's own layout system defines four breakpoint tiers — Mobile 360, Tablet 768, Desktop 1024, Wide 1440 — with layout changes at every boundary, including a navigation pattern change at 768. Two of the four tiers a theme will actually render in cannot be previewed.

The product grid renders properly, with names and prices. What it never says is whether those products arrived from WaWi, when they last synced, or whether this is sample content — the same origin question as S1-04, in the place a merchant is most likely to ask it.

What to fix, in order

Five things, ranked by what they cost to get wrong

1

Compliance can't be optional

A merchant can finish setup and publish a storefront with no consent management, no legal notice, and a silently inherited German tax configuration. Add legal and market to the checklist as non-deferrable steps, and gate publish on them.

S1-01 · S2-01 · S3-06 · W3.1–W3.6

2

Scope and inheritance are invisible

Theme edits and the publish action give no indication of what else they touch. Fix before multi-shop merchants arrive, or the cascade becomes a liability rather than the feature it was meant to be.

S3-01 · S3-02 · W2.2–W2.4

3

No undo on consequential actions

Role changes, 2FA changes and theme publishes all commit on a single click with no reversal path anywhere in the build.

S1-03 · S3-03 · H3.2 · H3.5

4

The ERP is invisible

No screen says where data comes from or when it last arrived. WaWi centrality is the platform's core premise and its least represented idea in the interface.

S1-04 · S2-02 · S3-10 · W1.1 · W1.3

5

The AI panel is unfinished

A prompt field with no submit control, no progress state and no result summary — on the one interaction the specification treats as a differentiator.

S3-04 · H1.2 · H1.4

Method and limits

How to read this

Three things that qualify these findings

  • One evaluator. Nielsen's method assumes three to five independent reviewers. A single pass reliably surfaces severe problems and unreliably confirms the clean ones — treat the Good ratings as the least trustworthy column.
  • Static screens can't show error states. Thirty-six criteria — most of H9, plus the WaWi overwrite behaviours — are marked not assessable rather than guessed at. They need a working build.
  • Two builds, small differences. Evidence here comes from rendering the local prototype, which labels the publish action Save / Publish. The screenshots on the Figma board come from a slightly later build that labels it Apply to storefront and shows a different demo shop. Where the two differ, the difference is itself noted as a consistency finding.
