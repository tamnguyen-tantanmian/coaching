Opportunity Map O1 · Nothing reaches customers unchecked

Opportunity solution tree · outcome → opportunities → solutions → assumption tests · 19 Sep 2026

Hypothesised, not researched. An opportunity solution tree is normally built from interview snapshots. This one is not — discovery has had zero merchant contact, so every opportunity below is derived from the build, the Pflichtenheft and the heuristic audit. gap means the spec requires something the build does not do — confirmable by reading, no merchant needed. need? means it assumes merchants experience that gap as a need, which nothing yet confirms. Every item here is a gap; none is yet a confirmed need. Treat this as a map of where to look.

Opportunities are short need phrases in the merchant's terms — verb first, four to ten words, with a without… qualifier where the condition matters. Reference shape: make local groceries affordable · buy local food without changing their existing shopping routine · help consumers live more sustainably. Two rules keep them honest: no barrier in the statement (it goes in the evidence line underneath — that is what separates these from problem-statements.md, which uses [user] needs a way to [goal] because [barrier]), and no solution in the statement — “know what's still missing” is an opportunity; “see a readiness checklist” is SOL-2 wearing an opportunity's clothes.

Why OPP-A is the target

1 · It is the only branch that fails silently. A merchant notices a wrong preview, an accidental publish, a publish that seems not to land. A's failure surfaces weeks later via a customer complaint or a regulator — by which point the exposure has happened.
2 · It carries the only legal consequence. P1 scored 20/20 and is the heuristic audit's own #1 of 32 findings — two methods reaching the same answer independently.
3 · It is what the outcome is named after. B, C and D make publishing trustworthy. A makes it correct. Without A, solving B–D produces a merchant who is confidently non-compliant.

Four competing solutions for OPP-A

OPP-A ★ target

Know what's still missing, without working it out myself

These compete — different moments, different mechanisms. SOL-1 and SOL-2 can combine; SOL-3 is the only one that addresses the root; SOL-4 is the weakest alone.

SOL-1 · Pre-flight gate at publish

Apply to storefront runs a check, lists what is unmet, and refuses to publish while a mandatory element is unset.

Also solves
C1 and C2 come free — a gate turns publish into a decision with a confirmation
Doesn't solve
A merchant reaching the gate with six things unset has still wasted the setup; says nothing about which market's rules apply
Blocking question
A gate implies the platform vouches for legal readiness. Who carries that liability? W3.6 is rated Needs fixing precisely here. Needs a legal answer before a design one.
AT-1 — assumes merchants accept being blocked rather than working around it. Add the gate to a prototype, run 6 moderated go-live tasks, watch for workaround-seeking. Medium cost.

SOL-2 · Non-deferrable checklist items

Extend the five-item checklist with the legal and market steps, marked required, so the gap never opens.

Also solves
A2 directly — required vs optional becomes a visible property of the list
Doesn't solve
Completion pressure. The audit's caveat on A6: deferring a domain is sound, deferring a legal requirement is not — but a longer mandatory list is what A1's completion bet is exposed to
Note
Cheapest of the four, and the one that could ship first
AT-2 — assumes a longer mandatory list doesn't depress completion. Split completion rates by list length in the prototype; compare against A1's ≥60% placeholder. Low cost.

SOL-3 · Market-derived requirement set

Collect country/market at shop creation, and derive the required element list — plus currency, tax treatment and locale — from it, per shop.

Also solves
A3 at the root, C1's currency/region-group divergence (§8.12), part of C2's locale problem, and S2-01's silent German default
Doesn't solve
Anything alone — it produces a correct list that still needs SOL-1 or SOL-2 to be shown
The real argument
SOL-1 and SOL-2 both assume someone hand-maintains a list of what DE, AT, CH and GB require. SOL-3 is the only version that survives the second market.
AT-3 — assumes market can be collected without breaking the three-field moment. Add the field and re-time against A4's 15-minute placeholder. Separately, ask engineering C1's question: what happens when a merchant picks USD on a EUR-priced catalogue? If the answer is "labels change, numbers don't", SOL-3 is mandatory, not optional. Low cost.

SOL-4 · Persistent readiness panel

A "what this shop still needs" surface on the Shop Admin shell, updating as configuration lands.

Also solves
S1-08 — the dashboard being laid out for the returning operator rather than the merchant still in setup
Doesn't solve
Anything, if merchants don't read it. The solution most dependent on B4 being wrong.
AT-4 — assumes merchants read an ambient panel they were not sent to. Instrument it — or cheaper, infer from B4: merchants who believe compliance is handled have no reason to look. Low cost.

The one question that decides the shape

B4, verbatim, to eight merchants: “What makes this shop legally ready to sell in Germany?”

They point at the tax toggle, or say “the platform does it” They will not seek this information out. SOL-4 is dead and SOL-1's gate is required — only an interruption reaches someone who isn't looking.
They name consent, legal notice, withdrawal and unit pricing They are already looking. SOL-2 plus SOL-4 may be enough, without blocking anyone.

One question, eight merchants, and it selects between a blocking gate and an ambient panel. Run it before designing either.

Deliberately not on this tree

Undo (C3) — belongs to outcome O2, where undo is the whole point. Listed as an opportunity because it shapes the branch; solving it here would duplicate that work.
The AI “Shop Lead” — a readiness check is one of the two shapes A7's assistant could take. Until A7 has an owner and a date, designing the check as an AI surface would pre-decide it.
Onboarding and Create — except A3, which has to be here because the market must be collected at creation to be derivable later.
Numbers — A1's 60%, A4's 15 minutes: placeholders, both. Real 5.0 baselines are still one of the four things blocking discovery. Present the indicators; leave the targets blank.
