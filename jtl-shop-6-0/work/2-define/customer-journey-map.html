Customer Journey Map Setting up a shop on JTL-Shop 6.0

Current state · grouped from the JTBD board, the prototypes, the heuristic audit and the A/B/C assumption sets · 19 Sep 2026

Shop owner  ·  User 1

Owner-operator or e-commerce manager, 1–50 employees, DACH, migrating from JTL-Shop 5.0. Non-technical by the spec's own description (§3.1). Buys on ROI and time-to-value.

Scenario: has just bought JTL-Shop 6.0 and logged in for the first time. The products already live in WaWi. Wants a storefront that looks like the brand and can take real orders — without calling a service partner.

Secondary — Shop staff (User 2): invited into a shop someone else set up; needs to know what is theirs to touch. Shown in the violet band.

Expectations

  • See every step between this login and a live shop, before starting
  • Connect the ERP on day one and work with the real assortment, not demo data
  • Put logo, colours and type on the storefront without opening a design tool
  • Be told the shop is genuinely ready — before it takes real traffic

Read this as a hypothesis, not a finding. Discovery has had zero user contact — it is the Pflichtenheft plus one desk article — and the test stage is a single-evaluator audit. The jobs are team-authored and unvalidated; the quotes are inferred from audit findings, not merchant research; the durations are design targets from the A-series signals, not measurements. What the map does show reliably is where the unvalidated weight concentrates — and that is Setup, by a wide margin.

S1-04 / S1-06No last-sync time, no sync health, no origin marker. The home screen cannot answer “is my data current?”

1 · Setup is four jobs wearing one label

Branding, commerce verification, permissions and compliance all sit under Setup — each with a different owner, failure mode and spec section. Five of sixteen owner jobs, six of seven staff jobs, and the densest audit cluster land in one column.

Splitting it is probably the single highest-value change to the board itself.

2 · Three jobs have no surface at all

Readiness check before go-livePublish — nothing exists
Know what an update changes firstManage — nothing exists
Invite & scope a colleagueSetup — not on the checklist

These are not usability problems. They are missing features the JTBD exercise surfaced and the prototype does not answer — which is exactly what a current-state map is for.

3 · The permission model is assumed by six jobs and built by none

Every staff job depends on scope being legible at the moment of editing. W2.2 — “edit states its scope” — rates Needs fixing on the theme editor.

Until roles get a home in Setup (define access) and Manage (maintain it), the staff lane maps a journey nobody can currently take.

Four decisions that need an owner and a date

  • A7 — is the AI “Shop Lead” on the MVP critical path? ⚑ ask first
  • Roles & permissions in MVP — yes or no
  • Theme vs preset — confirmed as preset, needs sign-off
  • Light/dark in the data model

Two tests that cost almost nothing

  • B4 — ask eight merchants, verbatim: “What makes this shop legally ready to sell in Germany?” If they point at the tax toggle, a legal group is MVP.
  • B5 — take ten representative JTL catalogues and try to express their tax treatment with the current control.

The shape to notice

The peak is Create and the trough is Setup — and they are adjacent. The product's best moment is immediately followed by its worst, and the drop is not gradual.

Everything that makes Create feel good (three fields, sensible defaults, permission to defer) is the opposite of what Setup does.
